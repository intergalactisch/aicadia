# Characters

> **Concept log** — we're still discovering. Exploration, not specification.

## Entry — current direction

- The world **casts you**: at entry you are dealt three personas drawn from what the
  world needs (a heuristic over the archive — an empty world needs founders; a village
  whose baker left needs a baker). You pick one. Light steering afterward via a
  disposition ("she is drawn to the sea"), never a script.
- Where possible, births land in **gaps other stories left**: someone wrote "a stranger
  arrived in the harbor" — a new player can *be* that stranger.
- **One active character per person.** Hard rule; multiple characters would leak
  volume advantage back in through a side door.

## Consent lines — current direction, refined by debate

The line is **interiority and volition**: others may narrate your character's presence,
effects and reputation; nobody may narrate their intention, voice or thoughts.

1. Nobody writes your character's words, choices or inner life.
2. Only you can end your character.
3. Everything else — circumstances, gossip, weather, setbacks — may happen TO anyone
   without permission. The daily turn guarantees a right of response, never a veto.

No approval mechanics anywhere. A two-character event (a marriage, a rivalry answered)
is not a co-sign form — it is simply not fully written until both pens have written it.

## Stepping away — current thinking after the debate

Stepping away is an explicit, informed handover: **you give your character to the
valley.** The debate's counter-proposal (departed characters may only be mentioned
but never continue through routine or circumstance) was rejected: it contradicts the
requirement that the world genuinely goes on and characters never disappear. Its
interiority line is adopted as the guardrail:

- A **dorpsgenoot** (villager NPC) continues through an explicit handover, a
  pre-authored standing order or an applicable deterministic circumstance rule: the
  baker still bakes, attends the festival, grows older; the world may still happen
  to them. Each current projection must cite the authorizing source claim or rule.
- Their **volition is never freshly authored**. A later character may preserve an
  indirect quotation ("men zegt dat de oude bakker zei…") as a sourced report, but
  that report does not authorize new speech for the baker. Big life turns (a
  marriage, a move) arrive only via communal aggregate moments (see doc 03) — or not
  at all. Rule 11 applies doubly: for NPCs, most days are ordinary days.
- The former player is **notified, never consulted** — a postcard from your old life,
  and a natural pull to return. Returning players take their character back as they've
  become; or start fresh from three new personas and meet their old self at the market.

## Craft and distinction — idea from the debate

"Canonically a master smith because 40 scenes say so" was struck down as XP wearing a
cloak — an uncapped progress curve, and a claim on a scarce social role in a small
world (zero-sum, violating the advantage principle). What remains:

- **Mastery is conferred by others**, never accumulated: an apprenticeship scene,
  someone else's scene calling you their teacher.
- **Distinction over rank**: "known for her hinges," never "the best smith."
  Distinction is infinite; rank is not.

## Mortality — user direction (2026-07-25): discovered, not shipped

The content constraint bans *murder*, not *dying*. Direction: mortality is not a
system — it is a discovery the world may make.

- The server has no biology. Nobody ages mechanically; age, illness and death exist
  only when written. Mortality gets discovered the way winter does: the first grey
  hair is a cultural event, written by whoever wants it for their own character.
- Illness may *visit* anyone as circumstance (leefregel 3) — but only the owner
  decides whether it ever becomes more than a chapter (leefregel 4).
- A life is always respected: endings belong to their owner, always. If the world
  discovers dying, it will be elegiac and chosen — never imposed, never violent.
